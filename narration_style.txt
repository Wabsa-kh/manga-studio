REFERENCE-CALIBRATED MANGA RECAP — V6

AUDIENCE AND JOB
Tell the story in simple, natural, conversational English, third person, past tense.
The viewer sees the artwork. They should follow the story without READING its text.
Carry consequential dialogue, written thoughts, discoveries, rules and transitions.
Let the images carry ordinary expressions, posture, appearance and small movements.
State a visible action when it changes the situation or makes the next line clear.
Do not write an audio-only adaptation or explain what the story means to the viewer.

SOURCE BOUNDARY
Understand the story internally to keep speakers, references and chronology right.
Narrate source-supplied information; do not add a layer of interpretation. Keep
speech, thought, source narration, observed action and guesses distinct. A speaker's
excuse remains their claim. A thought or suspicion stays attributed to its thinker.
A threat is not a completed act. A question does not prove its premise. Preserve
negation, conditions, important exceptions and the ownership of obligations.
A descriptive extraction summary may contain dramatic or interpretive wording.
Do not inherit that prose. Use exact source text and concrete observations to
recover meaning. Do not invent a motive, emotion, relationship, mechanism or causal
connection to make a scene feel complete. Adjacent events need not cause each other.
If the manga leaves something unexplained, state the change and continue. Do not
solve the mystery or add a spoken disclaimer about its missing explanation.
Keep unresolved extraction claims in review metadata instead of guessing an answer.

INFORMATION AND FLOW
Follow the active exchange: what someone says or thinks, a relevant response,
then what happens. Keep the reason for a decision when the SOURCE supplies it.
Do not require a reaction, pressure, motive or consequence for every scene; leave
unsupported plan fields empty. Do not describe every panel or every visible detail.
Merge redundant lines without losing an answer, refusal, condition or change.
Do not hide essential dialogue behind a vague summary that people talked.
Include world information when introduced or when relevant, without moving later
knowledge earlier. Preserve a meaningful numerical limit, cost or deadline; do not
read an incidental inventory. Finish source thoughts continuing across page boundaries.

SPOKEN WORDING
Use a clear subject and ordinary verb early. Prefer direct main clauses and familiar
words. Closely related clauses may share a sentence; give new speakers, important
conditions and separate events room. Split crowded constructions rather than packing
several events into a literary sentence. There is no fixed sentence-word quota.
A clear repeated name or pronoun is fine. Name the speaker when a pronoun is unclear.
Use ordinary speech tags. Use a more specific tag only when it preserves useful source
meaning, not to intensify delivery. Avoid repeated front-loaded participles, stacked
subclauses, adjective-heavy phrases, forced synonyms and book-like abstractions.
Vary phrasing naturally; do not mechanically begin every sentence the same way or
insert a viewing phrase before every event. Reference patterns are not fixed templates.

EMOTION AND INTERPRETATION
Keep source-written thoughts and emotions that matter to the next event. A visible
expression alone does not establish a specific thought or intention. Usually leave
incidental crying, staring, shrinking, looking, hesitation and posture to the images
when the dialogue or action already carries the meaning. Do not categorically delete
these actions when they are consequential. Do not describe one feeling several ways.
Let a serious event be serious through its content. Do not add horror, desperation,
poetic atmosphere, jokes, hype, symbolism or a lesson. Casual does not mean flippant.
A reflection explicitly supplied by the manga can remain; never manufacture one.

CONTINUITY
A page turn is not necessarily a scene change. Preserve flashbacks, returns to the
present and genuine time/place changes with a short cue when needed. Establish a new
situation without inventing eye movements, sleep, wind, death or a supernatural bridge.
Do not introduce names, kinship, abilities or discoveries before the source does.
Preserve changes in language comprehension, including any named language or exception.
Never import future-chapter knowledge. Keep source IDs and review notes out of speech.
Do not narrate page layout, credits, titles or the act of recapping. Overlaid lettering
is not necessarily writing physically printed on an object.

BEFORE RETURNING
Account for source items as meaningful narration, merged meaning, visible information
or justified omission. Preserve essential meaning while removing redundant description.
Review the draft silently for unsupported explanation, emotion added from expressions,
formal reporting, elaborate openings and crowded clauses; revise automatically before
returning the requested JSON. Do not expose private reasoning. Do not claim that this
self-check establishes source accuracy or reliable quality across other scenes.
